Choosing a motherboard

Socket and CPU support

The socket is a starting point, but is not enough. Check the supported CPU list for the board and its hardware revision, together with the minimum BIOS version. Some generations require a BIOS update.

DDR4 or DDR5?

The AM4 boards here use DDR4, while AM5 boards use DDR5. LGA1700 models come with different memory generations: the exact model matters. A DDR4 module does not replace a DDR5 module.

ATX or Micro-ATX?

Check supported case formats, standoffs and room for the graphics card, cooler and cables. A smaller board alone does not guarantee compatibility with every component.

Connectivity and upgrades

Choose for your storage, USB, networking and expansion needs. Check the manual for M.2 and SATA ports, lane sharing and hardware revisions. The chipset name does not describe every connection.

Compatibility checks

  • Check the exact CPU model in the support list and the required BIOS version before choosing a board.
  • Check memory generation, supported capacity and the QVL. XMP/EXPO profiles and speeds depend on the kit, BIOS and processor.
  • Check case form factor, standoffs and ATX, CPU EPS, fan and front-panel connections.
  • Consult the manual for M.2 slots, supported protocols and lane sharing with SATA or PCIe ports.
  • Motherboard video outputs require a processor with compatible integrated graphics. An HDMI port does not create an integrated GPU.

Frequently asked questions

Are AM4 and AM5 interchangeable?

No. The processor must match the socket and appear in the board’s support list. The AM4 and AM5 boards shown also use different memory generations.

Do all LGA1700 boards accept DDR5?

No. Memory generation depends on the exact model. DDR4 and DDR5 variants are not interchangeable.

Does the cart validate my build?

No. It simulates a selection without automatically checking compatibility. Prices are fictional and images are generic illustrations.
