Graphics card problems: symptoms and troubleshooting steps

A black screen, visual artifacts or crashes can suggest a graphics card problem, but they can also come from the display, cables, drivers, RAM or power supply. Start with the symptom and change one thing at a time before deciding to replace your GPU.

Before troubleshooting: record what happens

Note your exact graphics card, operating system, driver version and the game or application involved. Does the problem appear before Windows starts, on the desktop, or only during a particular game? Did it begin after a driver update, a new component or an overclock?

  1. Restart normally and check whether the problem repeats.
  2. Check the monitor’s selected input and the display cable connections.
  3. Try another known-working cable or monitor if available, changing one item at a time.
  4. Return custom GPU overclocking or undervolting settings to their defaults.
  5. Record any error message and compare the same workload after each change.

Stop using the PC if there is smoke, a burning smell or a visibly damaged power connector. Disconnect power if safe and seek qualified assistance; do not keep testing under load. Before touching internal components, shut down and unplug the PC and follow the component manuals. Never open a power supply.

Black screen or no signal: check the display path first

Confirm that the monitor is powered on and set to the input you are using. With a dedicated graphics card, the display cable normally connects to a port on that card. A motherboard video output only works when the processor and system support integrated graphics; moving the cable there is not a universal test.

If another cable, card output or monitor restores the picture, investigate the original display path before blaming the GPU. If the PC fails to start or its motherboard shows a diagnostic LED, consult the motherboard manual: a boot failure can involve memory, CPU, power or the graphics card.

A display that works during startup but goes black when the operating system loads can point to a driver or display-setting issue. Follow Microsoft’s blank-screen guidance for your Windows version, including recovery or Safe Mode where appropriate. A picture before Windows loads does not prove the hardware is fault-free.

After installing a new graphics card

Check installation and any required power connectors against the exact card and power-supply manuals. Modular PSU cables are not interchangeable just because they fit. Do not use a cable from another PSU unless the manufacturer explicitly confirms compatibility. If you are unfamiliar with internal installation, ask a technician rather than repeatedly reseating parts.

Colored blocks, flickering and distorted textures

These visual defects are often called artifacts. Try another display cable and monitor, then check whether the fault appears in one game or several applications. A game-specific rendering bug, unstable tuning, driver issue or hardware fault can produce similar symptoms.

Restore default GPU settings and verify the game files through its launcher. Check the publisher’s known issues and whether a recent update changed the behavior. A screenshot can provide a clue: a defect captured in it may be upstream of the monitor, while a clean screenshot can suggest the display path. Neither result identifies the cause on its own.

If artifacts recur across applications at default settings, especially before the operating system starts, document them and contact support. Avoid repeated stress tests on a system that is already unstable.

Game crashes, driver timeouts and unexpected restarts

Update the affected game and install a driver intended for your GPU and operating system from NVIDIA, AMD or Intel’s official support site. If the problem began immediately after a driver update, check the vendor’s known issues and supported rollback or reinstall procedure. Avoid unofficial driver-download sites.

Test at default settings without optional overlays or tuning utilities, one change at a time. If only one game crashes, investigate that game first. If multiple games fail or the entire PC restarts, other causes such as RAM instability or power delivery remain possible. A driver timeout or restart is not proof that the graphics card needs replacing.

Low FPS and stuttering are not automatically a GPU failure

Compare the game’s requirements with your complete configuration. Resolution, graphics settings, shader compilation, background tasks, CPU limits and memory pressure can all affect smoothness. Laptop power mode and GPU power limits also matter; a laptop GPU should not be treated as the equivalent desktop card.

Check temperatures and clock behavior during a normal session using appropriate monitoring tools. There is no single safe temperature for every GPU or sensor: use our GPU temperature guide to interpret readings. Lowering texture settings may help when video memory is a constraint; our VRAM guide explains why high allocation alone is not a diagnosis.

When should you seek repair or replacement?

Contact the seller, card manufacturer or a qualified technician when problems persist after simple checks, physical damage is visible, or repeated artifacts and shutdowns occur at default settings. Check warranty terms before disassembly or attempting thermal-paste and pad replacement.

Provide the exact model, driver version, symptoms, error messages, photos or screenshots and the checks already completed. Cross-testing a card in a compatible known-working system can help isolate a fault, but requires checking installation and power compatibility. It is better handled by a technician if you lack the equipment or experience.

Do not buy a replacement until you have a reasonable diagnosis. If a replacement is appropriate, our graphics card buying guide covers compatibility and performance comparisons. The NEXRIG graphics card catalogue uses fictional demonstration prices and offers no real sales or repair service.

Frequently asked questions

Do stopped fans mean the graphics card is broken?

Some cards stop their fans at idle. Check the documented fan-stop conditions for your exact model. A fan that fails to run when required needs investigation.

Can reinstalling the driver fix every graphics card problem?

No. It can help with some software issues, but cannot repair a damaged connector, faulty memory or another hardware defect.

Does a black screen mean the GPU is dead?

No. Check the monitor, input, cable and startup behavior first. The same symptom can have several causes.
