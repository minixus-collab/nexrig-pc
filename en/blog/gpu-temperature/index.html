GPU temperature: how to monitor it and reduce heat

A graphics card warms up when it works. To interpret its temperature, identify the reported sensor, workload and exact model. A single number cannot establish overheating.

SensorsMonitoringReducing heatGetting support

GPU, hotspot and memory temperature: what is the difference?

Monitoring software can report several measurements. GPU temperature is the main sensor or measurement exposed by the driver. Hotspot refers to a detected hot point on the chip, where available. Memory junction temperature concerns video memory on cards that expose that sensor.

These readings are not interchangeable. A hotspot higher than the main reading is not proof of a fault on its own. Cards do not all provide the same sensors or have the same limits.

What is a normal graphics card temperature?

There is no single threshold for every card. Consult the manufacturer’s documentation and support for your exact reference and sensor. Ambient temperature, case, cooling system and workload affect the readings.

Some cards stop their fans at idle when their operating conditions allow it. During gaming, temperature may rise and then settle. Compare your card against itself under repeatable conditions rather than a number reported for another model.

How to monitor your graphics card temperature

Use driver monitoring tools where they offer these readings, or an established tool such as HWiNFO downloaded from its official website. Identify the sensor name before comparing values.

  1. Record the exact model, sensor, approximate ambient temperature and game settings.
  2. Observe idle operation, then a normal session in the same game and scene.
  3. Record settled temperature, fan speed, clock speeds and any thermal-limit indicators.
  4. Change one setting at a time before measuring again.

A clock-speed reduction can have several causes, including power limits, changing workload or thermal throttling. Check the available indicators rather than attributing every FPS drop to temperature.

Reduce heat by starting with simple checks

Check airflow

Keep PC intake and exhaust paths clear. Check that cables do not obstruct fans and that installation follows the case manufacturer’s recommendations. Compare dimensions and fan mounting positions in our case category before considering a replacement; a new case does not guarantee a specific temperature reduction.

Clean filters and dust

Shut down, unplug and allow the PC to cool before cleaning. Follow manufacturer instructions, avoid liquids on components and prevent fans from spinning freely under an air jet. Do not open a power supply. If you are unsure how to proceed, ask a professional.

Limit unnecessary workload

An FPS cap appropriate to your usage can reduce GPU work when it otherwise produces unnecessary additional frames. Some graphics settings can also reduce load. Effects vary by game and configuration: measure the result rather than expecting a universal reduction.

Check fans and settings

Use the tool intended for your model to check settings. A more aggressive fan curve can increase noise. Do not disable thermal protections or modify the BIOS to bypass a limit. Replacing thermal paste or pads requires knowledge and can affect warranty coverage: consult the manufacturer before disassembly.

Our cooling category contains CPU coolers, not universally compatible GPU cooling kits. Improving CPU cooling does not replace diagnosing graphics card cooling.

For black screens, artifacts or crashes, use our graphics card troubleshooting guide before assuming overheating.

When should you contact the manufacturer or a technician?

Seek help for repeated shutdowns, artifacts, a fan that remains stuck under conditions where it should run, or persistent thermal throttling after simple checks. These symptoms can have other causes; they do not provide a remote diagnosis.

If you notice a burning smell, smoke or unusual electrical noise, stop using the PC and disconnect power if it is safe to do so. Do not continue a stress test.

Prepare the exact reference, sensor screenshots, game and circumstances. For a new or warrantied card, contact the seller or manufacturer before physical intervention.

Frequently asked questions

Is a stopped fan at idle a problem?

Not necessarily: some cards have a fan-stop mode. Check its operating conditions in your model’s documentation.

Does more VRAM mean less heat?

No. Capacity cannot predict temperature. Read our VRAM guide to understand its role.

Should you replace the graphics card?

Start by identifying the cause and checking support options. If planning a replacement, read how to choose a graphics card. Models in the GPU catalogue have fictional demonstration prices, with no real sales.
