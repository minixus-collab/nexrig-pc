How to choose a graphics card for your gaming PC

The right graphics card depends on your games, monitor and budget. Define your target resolution and frame rate first, then compare benchmarks with conditions close to your own setup.

This guide helps you compare models in our graphics card catalogue. NEXRIG is an educational project: its prices are fictional and no purchases are available.

Choose for your games and screen resolution

1080p: define your goal before comparing

A competitive game at reduced settings and a recent game with demanding effects place different demands on a GPU. List your games, preferred settings and frame-rate goal. When targeting high frame rates, consider the processor too: replacing only the graphics card does not guarantee more frames per second.

1440p and 4K: use benchmarks at the right resolution

At comparable settings, increasing resolution generally increases graphics workload. Do not infer 4K performance from a 1080p test. Check your monitor’s native resolution and compare cards at that resolution.

Monitor refresh rate, measured in Hz, and the FPS produced by the PC are different measurements. A 144 Hz screen does not guarantee that your PC will deliver 144 FPS.

How should you compare graphics card performance?

Use several independent tests that identify the game, version, processor, resolution and settings. Look at average FPS and, where available, consistency measurements such as 1% lows. Compare these figures only when the test methods are comparable.

  • Compare the same settings and resolution.
  • Distinguish native rendering, upscaling and frame generation: their figures describe different experiences.
  • Check whether ray tracing is enabled and whether you want to use it.
  • Look at several of your games rather than a single score.
  • Check noise and power measurements for the exact card if these matter to you.

How much VRAM do you need?

VRAM holds graphics data used by the GPU. Requirements vary with the game, textures, resolution and effects. More VRAM alone does not make a card faster: architecture and measured performance matter too.

For example, the GeForce RTX 3060 12 GB and GeForce RTX 4060 8 GB have different capacities. These numbers alone cannot tell you which to choose. Check benchmarks in your games with the texture settings you intend to use.

AMD or NVIDIA: compare features you will use

Compare performance in your workloads and whether your games or software support the features you need: upscaling, ray tracing, video encoding or creative acceleration. Check the exact model and software version; a feature offered in a product range may differ between individual cards.

Check compatibility before choosing

  • Case: check card length, thickness and remaining clearance with fans or a radiator installed.
  • Power supply: check the exact card manufacturer’s recommendation, required connectors and available cables. GPU consumption alone does not represent whole-system power.
  • Motherboard: check the PCIe slot and access to it. Performance can depend on the platform and available connection.
  • Monitor: check video outputs, the cable and the resolution/refresh rate supported by the complete combination.
  • Processor: find tests with a comparable platform, especially for competitive games at high frame rates.

Compare cases, power supplies and processors too. The PC builder helps organize a selection but does not certify all dimensions, connections or electrical requirements.

How much should you budget for a graphics card?

Budget for the whole system: GPU, processor, memory, storage, power supply and a monitor if needed. A more expensive card is not automatically the best fit for your games or screen.

For a purchase in Morocco, compare quotations and exact references from real sellers, including the total price, warranty terms and included items. NEXRIG amounts only demonstrate the catalogue and cart; they are not Moroccan market prices.

A simple selection method

  1. List three to five games and your preferred settings.
  2. Set your resolution and frame-rate goal.
  3. Compare two or three cards using comparable benchmarks.
  4. Check the exact model, compatibility and whole-PC cost.

Frequently asked questions

Is the card with the most VRAM the best?

Not necessarily. Memory capacity is one factor. Compare performance in your games and check whether memory becomes a limit at your settings.

Do you need to replace the processor too?

It depends on your system and usage. Comparable tests and observations of your PC can help identify a limit; a universal bottleneck percentage is not enough.

Does the same GPU mean an identical card?

No. Two cards using the same chip can differ in dimensions, cooling, connectors and power limits. Check the exact model’s specifications.
