How do you choose an SSD or hard drive?

SSD or HDD?

An SSD uses flash memory; an HDD uses mechanical platters. An SSD is generally useful for the operating system and applications, while a hard drive can suit large-file storage. Compare your requirements and cost per capacity rather than relying on a product range name.

NVMe and SATA: two protocols

The NVMe SSDs in this catalogue use PCI Express in the M.2 form factor. The SATA SSDs here use the 2.5-inch form factor. A form factor name alone does not determine the protocol: some M.2 SSDs use SATA. Always check both.

Advertised capacity and available space

Manufacturer capacities in GB and TB use decimal units. Your system may display different units, and formatting uses some space. Allow for applications, files and room for updates.

Beyond the marketing specifications

Performance depends on files, workload, temperature and platform. Check specifications for the exact part number and tests matching your use case. This catalogue provides no measured speeds, lifetime claims or commercial warranties.

What to check before adding a drive

  • For an M.2 SSD, check NVMe or SATA support, accepted length and mounting position. The 2280 form factor means 22 mm wide and 80 mm long.
  • For an NVMe SSD, check PCIe generation, lane count and boot support in the motherboard manual. An older slot can limit throughput.
  • For a 2.5-inch or 3.5-inch SATA drive, check the bay, mounting, data cable and SATA power connector. Do not assume all cables are included.
  • Consult port-sharing guidance: using an M.2 slot can disable some SATA ports depending on the motherboard.
  • Check cooling requirements, any heatsink and available space. Keep a separate backup of important files.

Frequently asked questions

Is every M.2 SSD NVMe?

No. M.2 describes a physical form factor; NVMe and SATA describe protocols. The drive and slot must match on both points.

Does a PCIe 4.0 SSD reach its maximum speed in a PCIe 3.0 slot?

No. An older connection limits bandwidth. Also check NVMe compatibility, lane count and BIOS support for your specific platform.

Why does 1 TB not appear as 1,000 GB in my system?

The manufacturer and system may use different units. Displayed space also depends on partitions and formatting; that alone does not indicate a fault.
